# V6 simulated-player playtest, 2026-11-09

Engine: local commit 03e5603, rules v6. Blue was a Sol 6.1 model making explicit choices from Fe's legal actions and forecasts; Red was the actual native Fe Easy opponent. No production rules, resources or opponent logic were changed. The local HTTP interface was used with separate action histories, without opening or changing browser checkpoints.

## Results and replay evidence

- Small: Blue capital victory on its eighteenth turn, after 17 completed full rounds. 36 Blue orders and 34 native Red orders, 70 actions total. [Replay](v6-sol-small.json).
- Medium: Blue capital victory on its thirteenth turn, after 12 completed full rounds. 26 Blue orders and 24 native Red orders, 50 actions total. [Replay](v6-sol-medium.json).

Each final export was replayed through Fe and its full JSON response matched the saved final response. Both comparisons were independently repeated against the unchanged local server. All 62 chosen Blue forecasts matched 19 exposed fields in the next native response, with no mismatches. These were complete matches, not test-budget draws or unfinished samples. Neither reached a campaign review point; boundary coverage remains from the directed automated tests.

## Findings

Small punished an early economic/specialist investment and an exposed crossing force. Blue recovered by defending and developing the bridge settlement, managing food and using explicit siege training at the stack cap. Exact attack forecasts mattered: equal attack and defense repels the attackers. Red took exposed armies and used diagonal routes.

Two Red attacks into the fortified bridge were repelled but damaged its defenders. One also relieved Red's food pressure. At zero-based Small replay index 46, five Red attackers killed two bridge defenders and reduced looming food losses. At index 59, one Red attacker killed one defender without an immediate follow-up. A forecast repulse is not by itself evidence of a bad strategic choice; alternative-action evaluation would be needed before declaring an AI defect.

Medium showed a meaningful two-crossing campaign. At zero-based replay index 45, Blue cut five Red holdings with an attack from tile 19 to 26. Red recruited at tile 20 and immediately retook corridor tile 19 at index 47, isolating Blue's expedition and causing one infantry attrition. Blue nevertheless had a two-order finishing route through tile 33 to the capital. Supply cuts mattered, but did not automatically stop every short finishing attack.

## Limits and next checks

This was model-simulated, full-information headless testing after reading project guidance. It does not validate browser rendering, first-time discoverability, human enjoyment, broad AI strength or beginner balance. Small was played first, so learning affects the later Medium result; the two victories do not establish that Medium is easier. One game per map and one opponent level are insufficient for difficulty calibration.

Next useful human checks: understand the kingdom-wide two orders and explicit computer handoff; read river/bridge and food/supply feedback without the manual; notice when a corridor can be recaptured; distinguish specialist recruitment from paid conversion; and save surprising decisions for review. Preserve the current challenging opponent while deciding later whether a gentler option is needed. Mutable bridges, construction materials, town specialties and new unit movement remain future design work.

Specific beginner guidance to assess in the browser: a forecast resolves one order, not safety against Red's two-order reply; Red may recruit before attacking. Check both orders' resource budget and the actual selected detachment composition. Cut-off forces can still move and attack: lost recruitment/income and incoming-turn attrition are the restrictions. These are proposed guidance improvements, not claims that this headless test evaluated the current browser's wording.
