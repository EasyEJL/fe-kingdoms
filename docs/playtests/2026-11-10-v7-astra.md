## Actual tactical differences

Rush massed starting infantry into the forest village, recruited twice, took the bridge in round 3, and built a village there for forward reinforcement. It is an infantry-tempo opening, not a prohibition on every economic action. First hostile attack was Blue's forest capture at zero-based trace 13, round 4: seven attackers against defense 4, three survivors. This cut Red's northern farm off from its capital. Red subsequently recruited at its hill village, attacked the forest unsuccessfully at trace 22 with eight infantry, moved five capital infantry into the hill village, then recruited and attacked unsuccessfully again at trace 27 with seven. Those attacks materially reduced both armies; the forecasts never promised that a defended tile would suffer no damage. Rush took Red's hill village at trace 29, leaving one survivor. It did not yet convert the economic advantage into victory.

Development spent eight initial gold on the safe home town, delayed forest capture by one order, captured the southern farm with a half detachment, and selected Arsenal at trace 8. It recruited one siege at trace 12 for four gold/three food, the discounted price, then merged infantry backward and moved the convoy forward at trace 16. That move consumed both orders. This rear-first specialist plan was expensive in orders: the Arsenal had saved only one gold on one specialist recruitment by the endpoint, versus its six-gold/two-food selection cost. It should not be described as an investment already paid back.

Red occupied the bridge during that preparation. Development's first hostile engagement was Red capturing its southern farm at trace 26 in round 7. Blue then attacked the bridge at trace 27 in round 8 with ten infantry/one siege, attack power 11 against defense 6, leaving four infantry/one siege. Siege gave no bonus against that unfortified, unsettled bridge, yet still cost both orders. Capturing the bridge cut off the Red-held southern farm, which lost one mobile troop at Red's incoming turn. The final legal list offers a siege assault on the enemy forest with power 6 against defense 4, but only one survivor and a full-turn cost. That attack was inspected, not executed.

The two lines shared identical Red replies through trace 11. Thereafter Red adapted to the different boards. Rush obtained contact/territory earlier; development retained more food and a specialist but yielded bridge initiative and a farm. These are observed differences between two adaptive plans, not a causal estimate of the town upgrade alone.

## Mistakes and constraints retained

- Development's rear Arsenal/convoy consumed setup and transit orders, while its lightly garrisoned farm was left exposed. Fortifying the forest at trace 24 did not protect that farm from Red's two-order bridge-to-farm sequence. The entire mistake remains in the trace.
- Rush repeatedly replenished the forward forest without fortification. Two repelled enemy assaults consumed substantial defenders. Its final acquisition leaves a thin frontier vulnerable to Red's remaining mobile stack. More ownership is not a demonstrated safe win.
- With only one order left, development's siege-containing moves disappeared from the legal list. It could not recruit and then make that move in the same turn. At the endpoint, its half-detachment alternatives omitted siege and cost one, while all-but-one moves included siege and cost two.
- No bridge construction, cavalry, campaign extension, terminal outcome, Hard opponent, Medium/Large map, concealed information, randomized starts or reversed seats were tested. No production policy was changed.

## Reusable opponent-version A/B protocol

The desired future experiment is **Fe opponent version A versus an actual improved version B against Astra**, not Astra Blue versus native Blue. No version B exists in this experiment and none was invented. An initially assigned native-Blue follow-on was canceled before it executed any tool, game action or write.

Useful preserved checkpoint candidates:

1. Rush replay prefix 20: start of round 6, Blue forest stack 7 versus Red hill stack 9, Red food 1, Red farm isolated. This tests food pressure, defense, and whether an improved opponent avoids or purposefully justifies the later sacrificial attacks.
2. Development replay prefix 23: start of round 7, Blue forest stack 10 including siege, Red bridge 2/forest 9, Blue southern farm exposed. This tests reinforcement, two-order pressure and tactical handling of the slow mixed army.

For one selected checkpoint, pin the exact source/binary of both actual policies and confirm identical reconstructed rules/state. Run 10 full rounds per branch initially, extending toward 20 only if authorized/budget-useful; stop earlier at terminal result. Keep map, seat, resources, legal rules and difficulty configuration fixed. Vary only the Fe opponent implementation. Record Astra's intent before the pair: e.g. preserve the forest supply choke, prioritize capital safety, exploit defensible forward recruitment, avoid knowingly losing attacks unless a documented follow-up compensates. Use the same Astra decision procedure and information access, but adapt to each actual board; do not force stale action sequences after policy divergence.

Record every actual decision, pre-action legal list, forecast, phase, opponent report, latency, casualties, territorial/supply/resource changes and terminal status. Independently mode 1-audit all chosen actions and final traces on the appropriate pinned binary. Preserve immutable prefix and full suffixes, all errors and mistakes. Compare matched round/phase endpoints rather than equal action counts when movement costs differ. Separate immediate forecast correctness from strength. Later testing should include different positions and both seats before general claims.

Consistent intent does not eliminate adaptive-Astra or learning/order confounds. A single paired result can identify a concrete better/worse decision or outcome, not establish overall strength. If a revision also changes rules, state representation or legal actions, it is not a policy-only A/B and needs an explicit compatibility/migration design rather than silently replaying the old checkpoint under new semantics.

### Resource-evaluation changes: intended future A/B use

This protocol is especially useful for a deliberate change to Fe's resource evaluation. Expect A and B to choose different actions quickly. Decision divergence is data, not a failure of the comparison. Record the first differing decision and subsequent resource/tactical trajectories from the same initial checkpoint with the same Astra seat, plan and information access. There is no version B tested here.

At each matched turn phase, record food and gold stock; gold income; food production, upkeep and net; supply connectivity; actual isolated and shortage casualties; settlement/specialty/specialist investments and their costs; territory, mobile forces, capital safety and terminal outcome. Track caps and discarded production only if directly measured from a validated transition, rather than inferring waste from a final stock of 40. Separate investment expenditure from combat loss and upkeep. Inspect whether a resource-saving decision postpones a necessary defense or useful attack.

Larger stock alone is not strength: spending can create a winning army, and high food may merely accompany loss of troops or opportunities. Judge whether the revised resource evaluation achieves better sustainable management while preserving tactical safety and useful progress. The current rush example has no army upkeep at the endpoint because every Blue troop is a lone garrison, not because it has demonstrated ideal force management. Adaptive Astra decisions remain a comparability limitation after paths diverge; use consistent intent, predeclared metrics and additional diverse checkpoints before a general improvement claim.
