# V7 model-simulated playtest, 2026-11-10

Source: frontier-expansion commit `0517958decec2496963682c48645323a8de2ce2c`, rules 7. Blue actions were explicitly selected by the model from actual Fe legal actions/forecasts. Red used actual native Fe mode 0: Small/Easy (depth 1) and Medium/Hard (configured depth 4). These were isolated, full-information HTTP games on localhost:8768, not automated Blue policy self-play, browser play, or human testing. Ports 8765-8767 and browser checkpoints were not touched. No production edits, builds or commits were made.

## Results

- [Small/Easy replay](v7-sol-small.json): ongoing at the agreed 120-action budget stop, 32 completed rounds. 57 Blue actions and 63 native Red actions. Both capitals survive. Blue recovered its original three holdings plus the forest town, with an 11-unit mixed army in the fortified forest Arsenal and five infantry in its Granary town. Red retains the eastern territory and constructed bridge. This is neither a victory nor an engine-declared draw.
- [Medium/Hard replay](v7-sol-medium.json): ongoing at the agreed 20-round stop, 79 accepted actions (39 Blue, 40 Red). Blue recovered its forest town with eight infantry and two forts; Red has nine infantry on the adjacent bridge and three each behind it. Blue's home Granary remains supplied. This is neither a victory nor an engine-declared draw.

Each exported trace was accepted unchanged by Fe. Two independent final mode-1 responses per match were byte-equivalent as parsed JSON. Exact intermediate choices, mistakes and native replies were retained, without resets or substituted Blue decisions. The replay JSON intentionally excludes ephemeral Red decision reports.

## What happened

Development-first survived, but did not produce a win in either bounded sample. Small invested in a forest town/Arsenal early, recruited cavalry and siege, then repeatedly lost corridors after moving its main stack away. Its late recovery restored both the Arsenal and a second town with Granary. Medium upgraded the home village first and chose Granary, expanded toward the northern crossing, lost exposed forward development, and recovered the forest town. These are strategic errors and contested fronts, not engine defects or evidence of an unwinnable map.

The shared bridge is genuinely shared. At Small trace index 27 Blue built site 12 from tile 11. It was initially neutral and empty, as forecast, and native Red subsequently occupied and developed it. Blue later captured it, lost supply and mobile troops, recruited there after reconnecting, and lost the bridge army to Red's two-order combination. Construction was useful to the opponent as well as the builder. No ownership/supply was granted merely by building it.

Permanent specialties mattered as durable assets. Small selected Arsenal at index 8, lost that town to Red, and recaptured it with Arsenal intact. Discounted cavalry/siege recruitment/training was legal at the expected reduced gold costs. Small selected Granary at index 109; Medium selected Granary at index 8. Their supplied production increased from five to seven food. Benefits depend on supply, so a captured or disconnected town is not a guaranteed economic advantage.

Movement cost is about the actual detachment, not the source tile's icons. Small used siege-containing two-order moves, and observed half-stack alternatives that omitted siege and cost one order. At Small index 24 it moved ten mixed units across the fixed bridge for two orders. Its next forest half-detachment included cavalry and also cost two, while a pure infantry half-move onto plain cost one. These forest alternatives were inspected, not all executed. Medium trained siege at index 44 and moved the mixed army at index 48 for two orders. This slower advance allowed native Red to reinforce and attack across successive replies. There was no executed cavalry-only forest move, and these samples do not isolate its balance effect.

Capture then fortify is a real two-order option. Medium captured tile 8 and fortified it at indices 4-5. Later it fortified a lightly held forward tile twice, but Red accumulated enough troops, captured it, and recruited there. Immediate forecasts correctly described each order; they did not promise safety from Red's next two actions.

## Forecasts and opponent feedback

All 96 chosen Blue actions were checked immediately against the next mode-1 native response for source/target troop counts, target owner, next side/orders, result and both food stocks, with zero mismatches. The final replay verification also confirmed all recorded Blue and Red actions occupied their exact accepted trace indexes. A second per-action replay audit passed all 96 actions against 22 resulting-state fields, including unit compositions, settlement, specialty, terrain/passability and both supply counts, with zero mismatches.

Actual native Red reports were recorded separately from replays. Their trace indexes/actions, configured depth, nodes, focus and immediate component sums were accepted by the adapter. Reports exposed actual shifts among advancement, reconnecting a holding and food support. Small's late Red food focus accompanied repositioning and a town upgrade, rather than a guaranteed future plan. Hard search horizon scores differed from immediate after-scores as documented. A positive highlighted component can coexist with a negative total immediate delta; it is a component highlight, not proof that the whole action improved immediately.

## Limits and useful next checks

One sampled game per map/difficulty, sequentially played with learning, is insufficient for balance or difficulty calibration. Neither reached a campaign decision or terminal result. Stopping was a testing-budget choice, not an engine capacity, round-limit or four-pass draw. No broad claim about development strength, AI optimality, browser readability or enjoyment follows.

The data was readable with protocol documentation, but this headless test cannot establish first-time visual understanding. Useful browser/human checks remain: distinguish construction from bridge ownership; see the exact moving composition before a costly order; notice permanent specialty transfer and supply suspension; understand that a one-order forecast does not cover Red's two-order reply; and distinguish an immediate component highlight from a deeper-search reason. No visual/browser validation was performed in this playtest.
