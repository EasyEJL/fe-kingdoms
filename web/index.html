Current v8 rules and strategy basics
  • You command Blue. Each side takes two orders per turn. Select a territory, then choose an order supplied by Fe and check its order cost. These are two orders TOTAL across your kingdom, not per territory. Selecting tiles is free. End my turn forfeits your remaining orders. An order can use one or two of that budget. Once your budget is spent or you End my turn, press Run computer turn to let Red act.
  • Move either half your troops (rounded down) or all but one to an edge-adjacent hex (up to six neighbors). Friendly territories combine troops and preserve their composition, up to 12 total. Fe reserves one garrison (infantry first, otherwise cavalry, otherwise siege) and chooses the moving composition proportionally from the rest; inspect the forecast before committing. Every departing army retains at least one garrison. Newly built bridges begin neutral and empty. A detachment containing siege uses 2 orders to move anywhere; cavalry uses 2 to enter a forest. Other moves use 1. Mixed forces pay the highest cost among the troops actually moving, never more than 2.
  • Base recruitment prices: 2 infantry for 3 gold and 2 food at a supplied capital, village or town. Supplied towns can recruit cavalry (1 for 4 gold and 2 food) or siege (1 for 5 gold and 3 food). A supplied town with at least one infantry can also Train it into cavalry or siege for the same cost and one order. Training converts the infantry and leaves the total troop count unchanged, including in a full stack. Fortify for 2 gold (maximum fort level 2). Build a village for 6 gold, then upgrade it to a town for 8 gold; each costs one order. A supplied town may choose one permanent specialty for 6 gold, 2 food and 1 order: Granary adds 2 food production per turn; Arsenal reduces cavalry/siege recruitment and paid training by 1 gold. Cut-off towns keep their specialty but receive no benefit; capture preserves it. Read Fe forecasts for actual local costs. Only orders allowed by Fe are shown.
  • All units have base attack and defense strength 1. A forest adds 1 defense if its defending army includes infantry. Each cavalry adds 1 attack on plains or farms, and each siege adds 2 attack against a fort, settlement or capital. Fe calculates attack power, defense, survivors and both resulting compositions in the forecast. An attack must exceed defense to capture. On capture, attacker losses are ceiling(defense * committed troop count / attack power), capped so at least one attacker survives. On a repelled attack, all attackers die; defenders lose max(1, attack power minus fort and terrain bonus), with at least one defender surviving. Losses remove infantry first, then cavalry, then siege, including attrition. Captured forts are removed. Use the displayed result rather than assuming troop count equals attack power.
  • Supply follows owned passable hexes to your capital. Mountains and rivers block it; fixed and built bridges carry movement and supply through owned connected territory; occupied bridges add 1 defense. From a supplied adjacent owned land bank, build a permanent bridge at a designated site for 6 gold, 2 food and 1 order. It starts neutral and empty, with 0 defense. Either side can occupy it; construction does not claim the tile or automatically connect supply. Supply counts above show owned territories connected to the capital or cut off; preview changes can include newly captured territories, not just reconnections. At the start of your turn, gain 2 gold plus the level of each supplied settlement, and 3 food plus 2 for each supplied farm. Gold and food are capped at 40. Food production is added and capped BEFORE upkeep is paid, so some production can be wasted at full storage. The projected food amount above comes directly from Fe. Villages are level 1 and towns level 2.
  • Supplied armies consume food for mobile troops: one food per started group of three troops above the lone garrison. Fe shows production, upkeep, net food and projected shortage losses above the map. A food shortage removes mobile troops from the largest armies. Cut-off territories instead lose one troop, to a minimum of one; they do not consume your central food.
  • Capture the enemy capital to win immediately. Four consecutive end-turn orders end the match in a draw. Small, medium and large maps initially allow 60, 120 and 180 complete rounds respectively. At the round limit, choose to extend by 20 rounds or accept a draw. The choice is recorded in the replay. A full replay pauses play for export; it does not declare a draw. All maps use the same two-order budget. Any non-pass order resets the consecutive-pass count.
  • Easy uses greedy one-ply evaluation. Hard uses four-ply beam alpha-beta search. Both use identical rules and resources, with no hidden bonuses. Select difficulty before a new game.
  • New games use kingdom-development campaign rules version 8. Version 1 square, version 2 hex, version 3 campaign, version 4 mixed-army, version 5 campaign and version 6 river and version 7 frontier replays can be viewed read-only and are never silently converted. Save replay downloads the full deterministic move record. Load replay validates it with Fe. A local checkpoint is saved only after a successful response. Export a replay before replacing a game if you want to keep it.
